        Zero has two jobs

        Adding zero changes nothing. Writing zero next to a number changes a lot. Here is why.

        exposure_zero

        Zero as an amount

        As an amount, zero means nothing. Put 7 apples together with no apples and you still have 7. That is why 7 + 0 = 7. Zero is the only number that never changes a sum.

        pin

        Zero as a digit

        As a digit, zero takes up a place. Every digit on its left gets pushed one place higher, so it becomes 10 times bigger. That is why writing 0 after 1 gives 10.

        A common trap: the number 101

        It is tempting to say “101 is 1 + 100, so the zero turned 1 into 100, a change of 99”. But that removes a 1 as well as the 0, so it measures two changes at once.

        Remove only the zero instead: 101 becomes 11. So the zero is worth 101 − 11 = 90, not 99.

        One zero at a time

        You can also ask what one zero is worth: remove just that zero and see how much the number drops. In 2,030, removing only the last zero gives 203, a drop of 1,827.

        Worked example

        How much is each zero worth?

        Zeros help each other, so their single values add up to too much. Averaging over every order gives each zero a fair share.

        Why not just add them?

        In 2,030, removing only the last zero drops the number by 1,827. Removing only the middle zero drops it by 1,800. Together that is 3,627, but all the zeros together are worth only 2,007.

        Each zero lifts the digits that the other zero also lifts, so they overlap.

        The fair way

        Put the zeros back one at a time, in every possible order, and give each zero the jump it causes. Average those jumps. This is called the Shapley value, from game theory. The shares always add up exactly to the total.

        Zeros after the decimal point

        Before the point, zeros make a number bigger. Right after the point, they make it smaller.

        makes it bigger makes it smaller changes nothing
          The rule

          A zero right after the point pushes every digit on its right one place smaller. So its value is negative:

          value = −9 × (what is on its right)

          In 0.05, the part on the right of the zero is 0.05, so the zero is worth −9 × 0.05 = −0.45. Check: 0.05 − 0.5 = −0.45.

          Zeros worth nothing

          Zeros at the very front (007) or at the very end after the point (2.50) push nothing, so they are worth exactly 0.
